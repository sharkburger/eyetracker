A PRACTICAL FIELD GUIDE

Start with the question. Then the specifications.

01 / LAB & SCREEN
Study attention on a screen

Define your area-of-interest sizes, whether you need saccade measurements, and whether the task needs real-time feedback. Check accuracy, precision, valid-data rate, timestamps, and stimulus synchronization. Validate after calibration with your own participants and task.

Explore: Spectrum, EyeLink, Fusion, GP3 HD

02 / REAL WORLD
Take research into the real world

Fit stability, slippage, prescription lenses, outdoor lighting, and scene video all matter. Plan dynamic area-of-interest annotation and gaze mapping in advance, and budget analysis time as well as recording time.

Explore: Neon, Pro Glasses 3, Dikablis, aSee

03 / INTERACTION & ACCESS
Turn gaze into interaction

For VR / XR, confirm that the platform exposes the data you need. For assistive communication, trial everyday postures, unintended selections, fatigue, and tracking recovery. Compare complete workflows and support services before choosing hardware.

Explore: Varjo, VIVE, PCEye, HIRU, IntelliGaze

About this review

Coverage

The atlas includes 50 devices and product families across screen-based research, wearables, VR / XR, assistive communication, consumer interaction, specialized integration, and selected legacy models. Family entries are included, so this is not a count of 50 distinct hardware SKUs. Coverage is broad, but not an exhaustive global inventory.

Sources and editorial assessment

Manufacturer product pages, developer documentation, and official discontinuation notices are prioritized. Introductions summarize public sources; selection notes are editorial analysis. No standardized hands-on comparison was performed, and no scores or rankings are presented as test results. Each record includes source links.

Limits of specification comparisons

Eye-tracking sampling rate, display refresh rate, and scene-video frame rate are distinguished. Maximum rates depend on configuration and operating mode. Unverified values are left blank. Accuracy tests differ in viewing distance, field of view, algorithms, and participants, so a single number is not a fair comparison.

Images, status, and updates

Product photographs, official illustrations, or promotional images are used with original-image links and credits. Rights remain with their respective owners. An official page does not establish current availability. Sources were checked on October 15, 2026; confirm current prices, stock, firmware, and licensing with the manufacturer.

Read the full methodology, limitations, and expansion scope

This edition is a catalog-style review of public information. Manufacturer websites and product and support documents were used to check names, applications, sampling modes, and product replacements. Accuracy, precision, and latency were not measured under common conditions. There are no rankings by sales or academic adoption. Access restrictions, redirects, and missing data sheets are flagged when further confirmation is needed.

Coverage does not exhaust historical SMI or Eye Tribe models, custom OEM modules, regional manufacturers, clinical nystagmus or vestibular systems, scleral search coils, EOG instruments, or webcam software platforms. Their omission does not imply that they do not exist. Future editions can expand these categories and add independent validation papers. Software platforms are not counted as standalone eye-tracking hardware.

For research procurement, request the exact model and configuration, raw-data fields and units, export licenses, SDK and OS versions, synchronization methods, calibration and validation results, total software and hardware costs, and the maintenance period. Assistive communication devices and research instruments have different evaluation priorities.
